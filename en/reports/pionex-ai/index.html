1 · What Pai suggests

Well-written technical analysis, no simulation

We asked Pai, the Pionex.AI assistant, for assets suited to grid trading and a specific configuration. It picked SOL and NEAR for their volatility and proposed ranges based on support, resistance, RSI and Bollinger Bands. Its market data was correct: the lows it cited (92.35 and 3.91 USDT) had been set that same day.

2 · The method, over two years

We repeated its reasoning 200 times

A single configuration says little: what matters is whether the approach works repeatedly. We turned Pai’s reasoning into a fixed rule, derived from its two suggestions, and applied it every week since late 2024, holding each bot for 30 days:

  • Lower bound: 8% below the low of the previous 30 days.
  • Upper bound: the high of those 30 days.
  • Geometric grid with ~1% between levels, stop loss 7% below the lower bound, 0.05% fee.

The outcome looks like a coin toss: a little over half the months end in profit, but the losses are bigger than the gains and the average is negative. One bot in four hits its stop loss, losing around 20%.

And the advice to enter when the price is in the lower third of the range makes results worse on SOL and doesn’t improve them on NEAR. When the price is near the monthly low, it’s often because it keeps falling.

3 · What Quant says about itself

AI-written backtests, never validated

Quant is Pionex.AI’s paid agent for strategy research. We asked it for the same NEAR backtest and it replied that backtest execution was disabled, without making up numbers, which is to its credit. Then we asked how it works. Here is a summary of its answers (quotes translated from Spanish):

  • For each request it writes a new Python script, which Pionex’s engine runs candle by candle.
  • It describes this as a Python model of the grid idea, not as the real bot.
  • It uses a single fee per trade, without distinguishing limit orders from market orders, and has no evidence that it calculates funding or liquidations.
  • On its accuracy versus real bots: I don’t have any validation figures.
  • It has no access to the user’s bot history.

4 · Is our simulator accurate?

Checked against 33 real bots

Asking for rigor means applying it to ourselves. We took the trade history of a real Pionex account (March 2025 to October 2026), rebuilt every futures grid from its orders and replayed it in GridReplay on Binance 1-minute candles, with the actual funding.

45 grids rebuilt. We left out 12 that were modified while running (investment added or range changed) because they aren’t comparable. That leaves 33: 18 long, 13 short and 2 neutral, on BTC, LINK, AVAX, HBAR, WLFI, XRP and others, with 2,298 filled orders.

Methodology and limits

What we measured and what we didn’t

  • Data: Binance 1-minute candles (spot for SOL and NEAR; perpetuals and funding for the validation), downloaded from its public archive and verified by checksum.
  • Within each minute we assume a conservative path (on bullish candles, low first and then high). Orders fill when the price touches their level.
  • The rule for Pai’s method is derived from two examples and was tested on two assets. Bots from consecutive weeks overlap, so there are about 25 truly independent periods per asset. It’s a clear signal, not definitive proof.
  • Pionex aggregates prices from several markets; small differences with Binance explain part of the deviations in the validation.
  • Past performance does not guarantee future results. This report is not financial advice or an investment recommendation.
